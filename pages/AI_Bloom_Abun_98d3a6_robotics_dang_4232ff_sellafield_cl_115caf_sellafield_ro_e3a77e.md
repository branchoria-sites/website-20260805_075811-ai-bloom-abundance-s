## Introduction

Quadrupedal "robot dogs" have become one of the most visible examples of how [robotics]({{ 'robotics/' | relative_url }}) is changing work at Sellafield. Their value is not that they replace nuclear engineers, but that they can repeatedly enter radioactive or otherwise hazardous buildings, follow the same inspection routes, collect consistent measurements and return without exposing people to unnecessary risk. This makes them particularly useful for routine monitoring, where the same rooms, stairways and plant areas must be inspected again and again throughout lengthy decommissioning programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>

Within the broader story of AI-enabled robotics, these machines are an early demonstration of how intelligent mobile systems can remove people from dangerous environments while improving the quality and consistency of data. Rather than promising fully autonomous clean-up, Sellafield's programme shows a more practical model: [human]({{ 'human-feedback/' | relative_url }}) experts remain responsible for decisions, while increasingly capable robots undertake repetitive inspection work that would otherwise consume time, protective equipment and radiation exposure budgets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>

## What robot dogs carry into radioactive buildings

The quadrupedal robots used at Sellafield are based on Boston Dynamics' Spot platform but have been extensively adapted for nuclear work through collaborations including Createc and other engineering partners. They are designed less as general-purpose robots than as mobile inspection platforms that can carry different sensing packages depending on the task.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>

## How repeated remote inspections reduce human exposure

The greatest advantage of a walking robot is not that it performs one dramatic mission. Its real value comes from repeating inspections reliably over months and years.

Many parts of Sellafield require regular monitoring because conditions evolve gradually. Equipment ages, contamination changes, structures deteriorate and decommissioning activities alter the surrounding environment. Consistent repeat surveys allow engineers to compare conditions over time rather than relying on isolated observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>

Repeated robotic inspections offer several operational benefits:

* **Lower radiation exposure.** Every inspection completed by a robot is one fewer occasion requiring personnel to enter controlled areas.
* **More consistent data.** Following similar routes and collecting information from the same positions improves comparisons between inspection campaigns.
* **Better planning.** Engineers can monitor trends before deciding when more intrusive work is necessary.
* **Reduced use of protective equipment.** Fewer entries into contaminated areas mean fewer disposable suits, gloves and other protective [materials]({{ 'scale-up-failure/' | relative_url }}) that later become radioactive waste.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>

This emphasis on repeatability is particularly important in nuclear decommissioning, where projects often extend over decades. Reliable inspection records become part of the engineering evidence used to plan future work safely.

## Why walking robots suit complex buildings

Traditional inspection robots often use wheels or tracks. Those systems remain valuable, but ageing nuclear facilities frequently contain obstacles that make wheeled movement difficult.

Quadrupedal robots can negotiate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* staircases;
* uneven concrete floors;
* cables and small debris;
* narrow walkways;
* changes in floor height;
* confined industrial layouts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>

</div>

This mobility means operators do not need to redesign inspection routes around the robot's limitations. Instead, the robot can often follow paths originally intended for human workers.

LiDAR mapping also allows repeated three-dimensional scans of the same spaces. Engineers can compare digital models collected months apart to identify physical changes, improving confidence in maintenance and decommissioning decisions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>

## Remote operation is moving beyond the site boundary

## New inspection tasks continue to emerge

As confidence in the platforms has grown, Sellafield has expanded beyond simple visual inspection.

One recent [development]({{ 'build-rights/' | relative_url }}) is a contamination-swabbing tool mounted on a quadruped robot. Surface swabbing is a routine [health]({{ 'health/' | relative_url }})-physics activity performed hundreds of times each day to monitor radioactive contamination. By automating this repetitive task, the robot can gather samples while reducing the need for staff to enter contaminated environments solely to perform manual swabs. The first successful trial demonstrated that the platform could carry out this specialised monitoring activity inside an active radiological area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-robot-swabbing-technology-trialled-for-the-first-time-at-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New robot swabbing technology trialled for the first time at Sellafield</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New robot swabbing technology trialled for the first time at Sellafield</span></span></span>

## Where walking robots still struggle

Despite their growing capabilities, robot dogs are not autonomous replacements for experienced nuclear workers.

Several practical limitations remain:

* Radiation can gradually damage electronics, limiting operating life in the most hostile environments.
* Complex industrial sites still contain spaces that require careful human judgement to navigate safely.
* Battery life [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}) the duration of individual inspection missions.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* Remote operation depends on secure, reliable communications.
* Unexpected obstacles or changing layouts can interrupt planned inspection routes.
* Engineers must still interpret inspection results and decide how decommissioning work should proceed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why this matters for AI-enabled human flourishing

Within the wider idea of AI supporting long-term human flourishing, Sellafield's robot dogs represent a grounded, present-day example of robotics removing people from dangerous labour instead of merely making existing work faster.

Their importance lies less in spectacular autonomy than in dependable repetition. By combining mobility, sensing, remote operation and increasingly intelligent inspection software, they enable hazardous environments to be monitored more frequently, with better data and less risk to human health. That pattern has implications well beyond nuclear decommissioning. Similar approaches could be applied to chemical plants, offshore infrastructure, disaster zones, mines and other environments where repeated inspection currently exposes workers to unnecessary danger.

The broader lesson is that AI and robotics can create value not only through dramatic breakthroughs but also through steady improvements that separate human expertise from hazardous physical presence. Sellafield's walking inspection robots show how that transition can begin with routine tasks, building confidence before more advanced autonomous capabilities are introduced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/case-studies/how-are-robot-dogs-helping-clean-up-sellafield" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">How are robot dogs helping clean up Sellafield?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Case study - GOV.UKJanuary 29, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2026</span></span></span>
