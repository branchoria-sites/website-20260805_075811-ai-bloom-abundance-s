## Introduction

The Bletchley AI Safety Summit marked the first major attempt by governments with very different interests to agree that advanced AI risks cannot be managed by one country alone. Held at Bletchley Park in the United Kingdom in November 2023, the summit produced the Bletchley Declaration, signed by 28 countries including the United States, China, the European Union and the UK, which recognised that [frontier]({{ 'compute-controls/' | relative_url }}) AI systems could create serious risks and that international cooperation was needed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-summit-2023-the-bletchley-declaration/the-bletchley-declaration-by-countries-attending-the-ai-safety-summit-1-2-november-2023" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Bletchley Declaration by Countries Attending the AI Safety Summit, 1-2 November 2023 - GOV.UKFebruary 13, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: november 2023</span></span></span>

Its importance was less that it created a global AI rulebook and more that it established a shared starting point. For the AI bloom vision — where advanced AI could accelerate science, improve [health]({{ 'health/' | relative_url }}), expand knowledge and help humanity overcome major constraints — coordination is essential. Powerful AI could become a shared tool for human flourishing, but only if countries can cooperate on safety, access and responsible [development]({{ 'build-rights/' | relative_url }}). Bletchley showed that cooperation is possible, while also revealing how difficult it will be to align nations that compete for technological advantage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-summit-2023-the-bletchley-declaration/the-bletchley-declaration-by-countries-attending-the-ai-safety-summit-1-2-november-2023" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Bletchley Declaration by Countries Attending the AI Safety Summit, 1-2 November 2023 - GOV.UKFebruary 13, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: november 2023</span></span></span>

## The rise of international AI agreements

Before Bletchley, [AI governance]({{ 'ai-governance/' | relative_url }}) was developing mainly through separate national strategies. Governments were creating their own approaches to regulation, research funding and industrial policy, but there was no widely recognised international forum focused specifically on the risks of the most advanced AI systems.

The Bletchley Summit changed that by bringing together governments, researchers, companies and civil society around a narrower question: how should the world respond to frontier AI — highly capable systems that may create new security, economic and social challenges? The Bletchley Declaration did not attempt to harmonise all AI laws. Instead, it created agreement around several broad principles: that AI should be developed safely and responsibly, that risks require international cooperation, and that scientific understanding of advanced AI should be improved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-summit-2023-the-bletchley-declaration/the-bletchley-declaration-by-countries-attending-the-ai-safety-summit-1-2-november-2023" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Bletchley Declaration by Countries Attending the AI Safety Summit, 1-2 November 2023 - GOV.UKFebruary 13, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: november 2023</span></span></span>

A notable achievement was the inclusion of both technological rivals and emerging AI powers. The participation of China alongside the US, EU members and other countries was significant because AI development is closely connected with geopolitical competition. Cooperation between major powers on any technology area is difficult, particularly when AI is also viewed as a source of economic, military and strategic influence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://time.com/6331320/us-china-ai-safety-summit/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: time.com">[TIME]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">time.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Why China&#x27;s Involvement in the U.K</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AI Safety Summit Was So SignificantNovember 3, 2023 — China&#x27;s participation in the U.K. AI Safety Summit was a landmark event, marking a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 3, 2023</span></span></span>

The summit also created a pathway for continuing scientific collaboration. Countries agreed to support an international assessment of the science of advanced AI safety, later developed through work involving an international expert group chaired by Yoshua Bengio. The aim was not to remove disagreement, but to create a stronger shared evidence base for future decisions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/international-scientific-report-on-the-safety-of-advanced-ai" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Scientific Report on the Safety of Advanced AI: interim report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">17, 2024...</span></span></span>

## What cooperation can achieve

International AI cooperation can help address problems that individual governments cannot solve alone. Advanced AI systems are developed through global supply chains involving chips, computing infrastructure, research talent and multinational companies. A safety failure or misuse of a highly capable system could also cross borders quickly through cyberattacks, misinformation campaigns or [automated]({{ 'auto-experiments/' | relative_url }}) exploitation.

One practical area where Bletchley influenced policy was AI testing. The summit encouraged governments to develop stronger capabilities for evaluating advanced systems before deployment. The UK’s AI Safety Institute was established to test frontier AI models, study their capabilities and provide evidence for policymakers. Its work includes evaluating risks such as cyber misuse, biological capabilities, autonomous behaviour and wider societal impacts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-institute-approach-to-evaluations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A I Safety Institute approach to evaluations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AI Safety Institute approach to evaluations - GOV.UKFebruary 9, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2024</span></span></span>

This approach reflects an important shift. Earlier technology governance often focused mainly on rules after products reached society. Frontier AI requires understanding systems before widespread deployment because the consequences of failure may be difficult to reverse. Independent testing can help governments make better decisions about whether a system is ready for release and what safeguards may be needed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/our-approach-to-evaluations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aisi.gov.uk">[AI Security Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aisi.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AI Security Institute AI Safety Institute approach to evaluations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AI Security InstituteAI Safety Institute approach to evaluations - GOV.UKFebruary 9, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 9, 2024</span></span></span>

Cooperation can also support the positive side of AI development. If advanced AI becomes a driver of scientific discovery, medical breakthroughs or productivity growth, international standards and shared research could help ensure those benefits spread more widely. A future where AI contributes to human flourishing depends not only on making systems powerful, but also on making their benefits [accessible]({{ 'accessible-tutors/' | relative_url }}) and their risks manageable.

However, cooperation does not automatically guarantee equal access. Countries with greater computing resources, stronger research institutions and leading AI companies may gain disproportionate influence over how advanced AI develops. The same technology that could expand human capabilities could also deepen existing power differences if governance is shaped only by the strongest actors.

The declaration encouraged governments to work together, but it did not establish a global regulator, binding safety requirements or penalties for countries and companies that ignore commitments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-summit-2023-the-bletchley-declaration/the-bletchley-declaration-by-countries-attending-the-ai-safety-summit-1-2-november-2023" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Bletchley Declaration by Countries Attending the AI Safety Summit, 1-2 November 2023 - GOV.UKFebruary 13, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: november 2023</span></span></span>

Some governments place greater emphasis on long-term catastrophic risks from highly capable AI systems. ## The Bletchley lesson for an AI-enabled human future

The central lesson of Bletchley is that [AI coordination]({{ 'ai-coordination/' | relative_url }}) is not simply a technical problem. It is a question of how humanity manages a technology that could increase collective intelligence while also increasing competition between powerful actors.

For an AI-enabled human bloom, international cooperation could become a foundation for using advanced AI to solve problems that exceed the capacity of individual nations. Shared safety research, better testing and open scientific exchange could help humanity capture benefits such as faster medical discovery, improved climate modelling and more effective responses to global crises.

But cooperation must address power as well as risk. A future shaped by AI should not depend only on a small group of governments or companies deciding how intelligence-enhancing technologies are developed and [distributed]({{ 'ai-access/' | relative_url }}). The long-term challenge is creating institutions that allow humanity to gain the benefits of increasingly powerful AI while preserving broad participation, accountability and human agency.

Bletchley was therefore a beginning rather than a solution. It demonstrated that countries can find common ground around AI safety, even amid rivalry. The harder task is turning that shared recognition into durable systems capable of guiding AI development towards widespread human flourishing.
