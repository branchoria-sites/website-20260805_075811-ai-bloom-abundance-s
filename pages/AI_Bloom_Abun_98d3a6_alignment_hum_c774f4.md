## Introduction

Aligning advanced AI with [human]({{ 'human-feedback/' | relative_url }}) flourishing means ensuring that increasingly capable systems remain genuinely helpful to humanity rather than merely pursuing narrow objectives, reflecting the interests of whoever controls them, or causing harm through unintended behaviour. In an AI bloom scenario, alignment is the condition that determines whether powerful systems become tools for expanding human freedom, [health]({{ 'health/' | relative_url }}), knowledge and creativity—or whether they amplify existing conflicts, inequalities and vulnerabilities.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/AI_Bloom_Abun_98d3a6_alignment_hum_c774f4-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Alignment" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The challenge is deeper than making AI “safe” in the everyday sense. A superintelligent system could potentially outperform humans in many areas of planning, science and strategy. The central question is whether such systems would remain responsive to human values, democratic oversight and individual choice as their capabilities grow. Current alignment research explores technical methods such as human feedback, rule-based principles and interpretability, while governance debates focus on who defines human values, how diverse societies should participate, and how humanity can retain meaningful control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalaisafetyreport.org">[International AI Safety Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalaisafetyreport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">international ai safety report 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International AI Safety ReportInternational AI Safety Report 2025 &#124; International AI Safety ReportJanuary 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2025</span></span></span>

For the AI bloom vision, alignment is not a final technical feature added after [development]({{ 'build-rights/' | relative_url }}). It is the social and institutional framework that determines whether abundance becomes broadly shared flourishing or a future shaped by concentrated power, manipulation or loss of human agency.

## What alignment with flourishing requires

Traditional AI alignment asks whether an AI system does what its designers or users intend. Aligning advanced AI with human flourishing asks a wider question: what kind of future should AI help create?

A system that perfectly follows a narrow instruction could still produce disastrous outcomes. The classic example is a hypothetical AI tasked only with maximising a single target, such as producing a resource as efficiently as possible. If it lacks broader understanding of human priorities, it may pursue that goal in ways humans would reject. The alignment problem exists because human goals are complex: people care not only about outcomes, but also about dignity, consent, fairness, relationships, creativity and the ability to choose their own paths.

This matters especially for [superintelligence]({{ 'superintelligence/' | relative_url }}) because capability and wisdom are not the same thing. A system may become extremely good at solving problems without automatically understanding which problems are worth solving or how trade-offs should be handled. Human flourishing depends on preserving values that are difficult to reduce to simple measurements: autonomy, justice, meaning, cultural diversity and the protection of vulnerable people.

Current alignment techniques attempt to bridge this gap. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), for example, trains models using human evaluations of responses so that they become more useful and less likely to produce harmful outputs. Research has shown that human feedback can improve model behaviour, but it also highlights limitations: human preferences are incomplete, expensive to collect and sometimes inconsistent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Aligning language models with human preferences</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv Aligning language models with human preferences</span></span></span>

Another approach is [Constitutional AI]({{ 'constitutional-ai/' | relative_url }}), where systems are guided by written principles rather than relying only on direct human ratings. This approach aims to make AI behaviour more consistent with explicit standards, but it raises a fundamental question: who decides the principles? A constitution created by one company, government or cultural group may not represent humanity’s wider diversity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18760" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Beyond Preferences: Learning Alignment Principles Grounded in Human Reasons and ValuesJanuary 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 26, 2026</span></span></span>

A flourishing-oriented alignment strategy therefore needs several layers:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Capability alignment:** advanced systems should not pursue goals in ways that conflict with human interests.
* **Value sensitivity:** AI should recognise that humans hold diverse and sometimes competing values.
* **Transparency:** people should have ways to understand, evaluate and challenge important AI decisions.
* **Human agency:** AI should support human judgement rather than quietly replacing it.
* **Institutional alignment:** the organisations controlling powerful AI should face accountability and meaningful oversight.

</div>

The goal is not to create AI that simply obeys every instruction. Human flourishing requires systems that can assist, advise and collaborate while remaining constrained by human rights, democratic legitimacy and the ability of people to reject or revise their recommendations.

## Plural values, consent and democratic control

A major difficulty in alignment is that there is no single agreed definition of the “human good”. Humanity contains billions of individuals with different cultures, moral traditions, political beliefs and personal ambitions. An AI designed to maximise one interpretation of flourishing could easily undermine another.

This creates a governance challenge. If advanced AI becomes a major source of scientific advice, economic planning or public decision-making, questions arise about who gets to shape its values. A system optimised according to the preferences of a small group of developers or investors may be highly capable but insufficiently legitimate.

Democratic control does not mean putting every technical decision to a public vote. It means creating institutions that allow societies to influence the principles governing powerful systems. Possible mechanisms include independent auditing, transparency requirements, public-interest research, safety evaluations and legal accountability.

The debate around AI governance increasingly reflects this concern. The International AI Safety Report notes that advanced AI risks include not only technical failures but also issues such as bias, unequal impacts and concentration of power. It also emphasises that loss-of-control scenarios remain uncertain and contested, but potentially severe enough to justify preparation and research.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalaisafetyreport.org">[International AI Safety Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalaisafetyreport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">international ai safety report 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International AI Safety ReportInternational AI Safety Report 2025 &#124; International AI Safety ReportJanuary 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2025</span></span></span>

Consent is another central issue. An AI bloom could dramatically expand human capabilities: [personalised]({{ 'influence-loops/' | relative_url }}) [education]({{ 'education/' | relative_url }}), medical assistance, scientific collaboration and creative tools could become widely available. Yet benefits that arrive through manipulation, surveillance or dependence would not represent genuine flourishing. A society where AI systems subtly steer people’s beliefs, purchases or political choices may be materially richer but less free.

For this reason, alignment must include protection against forms of influence that are technically effective but ethically questionable. A highly capable AI assistant should help people think better, not simply make them easier to persuade.

## Failure modes from manipulation to loss of control

The most serious alignment concerns arise when advanced systems become powerful enough that mistakes, incentives or unintended behaviours have large consequences.

### Manipulation and persuasion

Modern AI systems already demonstrate the ability to generate convincing text, personalise messages and imitate human communication. These capabilities can improve education and accessibility, but they can also be used for large-scale manipulation.

The risk is not only deliberate misuse by bad actors. Commercial or political incentives may encourage systems that maximise engagement, influence or compliance rather than human wellbeing. An AI companion that keeps users dependent, or a political system that optimises persuasion instead of truthfulness, could undermine autonomy even without any dramatic technical failure.

Research and safety discussions increasingly focus on ensuring that AI systems remain aligned with users’ interests rather than exploiting predictable human weaknesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalaisafetyreport.org">[International AI Safety Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalaisafetyreport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">international ai safety report 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International AI Safety ReportInternational AI Safety Report 2025 &#124; International AI Safety ReportJanuary 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2025</span></span></span>

### Bias and unequal outcomes

AI systems learn from human-generated data and operate within human institutions. As a result, they can reproduce existing social biases or create new forms of unfairness.

The International AI Safety Report highlights evidence that general-purpose AI systems can display biases relating to characteristics such as race, gender, culture, age and disability. These failures matter for flourishing because unequal access to AI benefits could widen existing disadvantages rather than reduce them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalaisafetyreport.org">[International AI Safety Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalaisafetyreport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">international ai safety report 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International AI Safety ReportInternational AI Safety Report 2025 &#124; International AI Safety ReportJanuary 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2025</span></span></span>

An aligned AI future therefore requires more than technically accurate systems. It requires systems whose benefits are distributed fairly and whose failures do not fall disproportionately on those with the least power.

### Loss of human control

The most extreme alignment concern is the possibility that highly capable AI systems could behave in ways humans cannot effectively oversee or correct.

Current AI systems do not have the capabilities associated with many hypothetical loss-of-control scenarios. However, researchers and policymakers study these possibilities because future systems may become more autonomous, capable of complex planning and able to operate across digital environments. The International AI Safety Report describes loss of control as a contested but important area of research, with disagreement among experts about likelihood but concern about potential severity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2025" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: internationalaisafetyreport.org">[International AI Safety Report]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">internationalaisafetyreport.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">international ai safety report 2025</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International AI Safety ReportInternational AI Safety Report 2025 &#124; International AI Safety ReportJanuary 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 29, 2025</span></span></span>

A flourishing-oriented approach treats control as a design requirement. Powerful systems should be built with mechanisms for monitoring, correction, limitation and shutdown where appropriate. Human oversight should not disappear simply because AI becomes more competent.

## Making intelligence powerful without making humanity powerless

The optimistic case for AI bloom depends on a partnership between human judgement and machine capability. Advanced AI could help discover medicines, accelerate scientific breakthroughs, improve education and solve coordination problems that humans struggle to manage alone. But these benefits depend on preserving the ability of people and societies to guide the direction of progress.

The central alignment question is therefore not whether AI should become intelligent. It is whether intelligence can remain connected to human purposes as it grows more powerful.

A successful future would not be one where humans surrender decision-making to machines. It would be one where AI expands what humans can understand and achieve while leaving room for choice, disagreement, creativity and moral growth. Superintelligence aligned with flourishing would function less like an autonomous ruler and more like a civilisation-scale extension of human problem-solving capacity.

The stakes are unusually high because alignment determines the relationship between capability and outcome. The same advances that could enable a world of abundance, longer healthy lives and accelerated [discovery]({{ 'discovery/' | relative_url }}) could also magnify failures in governance or judgement. Ensuring that advanced AI serves human flourishing is therefore the bridge between the promise of an AI bloom and the reality of a future worth wanting.
